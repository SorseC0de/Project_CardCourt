# The crew, measured

200 Standard games under the rules as they stand: three officials a round, dealt face up,
and none of them retiring when called. Run it with `./Tools/sim --refs`.

## Nobody is murdering the score

Held out one at a time, 80 games each against the whole crew's **19.05 PTS**:

| held out | PTS | swing |
|---|---|---|
| Goaltending | 19.10 | +0.05 |
| Technical Foul | 18.96 | −0.09 |
| Back Court Violation | 18.79 | −0.26 |
| Offensive Foul | 18.54 | −0.51 |
| Foot On The Line | 18.15 | −0.90 |
| Charge | 17.94 | −1.11 |

Every swing is inside a point, and **most of them are negative** — taking an official out
lowers scoring slightly rather than raising it. That is not the officials being harmless;
it is the crew slot always being filled. Removing one does not remove a call, it promotes
whoever was next in the deck. The scoreboard cannot see the difference.

So the answer to *is one of them murdering the soak* is **no**, and the reason the question
felt right is worth keeping: it is not the points they cost, it is how often they speak.

## How often they speak

Calls per round worked, over 200 games:

| referee | rounds | calls | per round |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical Foul | 248 | 243 | **0.98** |
| Offensive Foul | 245 | 232 | 0.95 |
| Foot On The Line | 255 | 231 | 0.91 |
| Charge | 256 | 221 | 0.86 |
| Back Court Violation | 260 | 218 | 0.84 |
| Extravagant Mechanics | 249 | 192 | 0.77 |
| Discontinued Dribble | 248 | 189 | 0.76 |
| Goaltending | 252 | 164 | 0.65 |
| Official Review | 257 | 152 | 0.59 |
| Flagrant Foul | 248 | 116 | 0.47 |
| Flagrant Foul II | 250 | 113 | 0.45 |
| Blocking Foul | 255 | 113 | 0.44 |
| Double Dribble | 240 | 99 | 0.41 |
| Over-Varing Evidence | 265 | 98 | 0.37 |
| Travel | 264 | 52 | 0.20 |
| Shot Clock Violation | 258 | 39 | 0.15 |
| Clear Path Foul | 241 | 34 | 0.14 |
| Delay-of-Game Warning | 257 | 26 | 0.10 |
| **Cleared to Play** | 252 | **0** | **0.00** |

**Technical Foul all but fires on sight.** It cancels the first non-Whistle card anybody
plays, and in 248 rounds of work it made 243 calls — which is a cancelled play in
essentially every round it is out, three rounds in nineteen. The suspicion was right about
the card and wrong about where it shows: it does not cost points, it costs a play.

The three shot-type calls sit right behind it at 0.86 to 0.95. A round with any of them out
is a round where somebody's finish is taken away.

**Cleared to Play never fires at all.** It answers an Injury turning up in a draw, and
Injuries are parked — so it is a blank standing in a crew slot for 252 rounds out of 200
games, which is one round in nineteen where only two officials are actually working.

Five more speak in under a quarter of the rounds they work: Travel, Shot Clock Violation,
Clear Path Foul, Delay-of-Game Warning, and Double Dribble at 0.41.

## What the numbers do not say

Points are the wrong instrument for this and the held-out table is the proof: nineteen cards
sharing three slots means no one of them can move the score. What a crew decides is the
*texture* of a round — how often a play is waved off, and whether the thing waved off was
the thing you had been building toward. That wants a different measurement than this one,
and it wants deciding what the target is first.
